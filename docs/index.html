The authors assembled and characterized over two thousand one hundred complete centromeres from a diverse group of individuals spanning five continental and twenty-eight population groups. They report finding hundreds of previously unknown centromere variants, and observed that mobile genetic elements appear in about thirty percent of centromeres. They also found that mutation rates vary fifty-fold across different chromosomes, with the region where the cell's division machinery attaches, called the kinetochore site, mutating fastest of all. The authors describe this as evidence of an evolutionary arms race between centromeric DNA and the proteins that bind it. This peer-reviewed paper has drawn nine citations in eight weeks.
